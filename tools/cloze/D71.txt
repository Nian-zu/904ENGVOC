## 英國的四天工作週實驗
In 2022, 61 companies in Britain tried something new. For six months their staff worked four days instead of five, so every [[weekend|midnight|noon|textbook]] was three days long. Nobody lost any pay. People worked about 34 hours on those four [[weekdays|midnights|noons|textbooks]], a little less than before. Researchers then asked what had happened. Most workers said they were less [[tired|salty|crowded|windy]] and had more time for their families and their own [[chores|coins|mirrors|towels]]. Fewer people quit their jobs, and fewer called in sick. The money side did not fall apart either: on average, company earnings stayed about the same. When the test ended, 56 of the 61 companies went on with the shorter week, and 18 of them made it permanent. It was the largest test of its kind so far. Even so, most British companies have not changed anything. Some kinds of work, like nursing or driving a bus, cannot simply be squeezed into four days. My father says he would take the deal at once; my mother says she would just finish her work at home on the [[weekend|noon|midnight|uniform]].
> 1 every weekend was three days long「每個週末都有三天」，少上一天班的結果。midnight、noon、textbook 都不能用「三天長」來形容。
> 2 on those four weekdays「在那四個工作日」。midnights、noons、textbooks 都不是上班的日子。
> 3 they were less tired「他們比較不累」。salty（鹹的）、crowded（擁擠的）、windy（風大的）都不能形容人的狀態。
> 4 time for their families and their own chores「有時間陪家人、做自己的家事」。coins、mirrors、towels 都不是會占用時間的事情。
> 5 finish her work at home on the weekend「週末在家把工作做完」。noon、midnight、uniform 都放不進這個句子。
+ staff = 員工
+ quit = 辭職
+ earnings = 收入
+ permanent = 永久的
+ squeeze = 硬塞
= 2022 年，英國有 61 家公司嘗試了新做法。六個月期間，他們的員工一週只上四天班，所以每個週末都有三天，而且薪水一毛都沒少。那四個工作日他們大約工作 34 小時，比以前稍微少一點。研究人員接著調查結果如何。大多數員工說自己比較不累，有更多時間陪家人、做自己的家事。離職的人變少，請病假的人也變少。金錢方面也沒有崩盤：平均來說，公司的收入和以前差不多。試驗結束後，61 家公司裡有 56 家繼續實施比較短的工作週，其中 18 家把它變成永久制度。這是目前同類實驗中規模最大的一次。即使如此，英國大部分公司還是沒有改變。有些工作，像護理或開公車，沒辦法硬塞進四天。我爸說他馬上就要這種待遇；我媽說她只會在週末在家把工作做完。

## 東京都也試四天工作週

That gives them a three-day [[weekend|noon|midnight|textbook]]. Japan's government is [[worried|salty|windy|crowded]] because fewer and fewer babies are born each year, and many people say the main reason is that parents, especially mothers, simply have no time. The governor said the aim was that nobody should have to leave a career because of childbirth or child care. A second new rule lets parents of small children work two hours less a day, with lower pay. The city government is one of the biggest employers in Japan, with about 160,000 staff, so the change is being watched closely. Workers still have to reach about 155 hours a month, which means some of them will work longer on the other [[weekdays|midnights|noons|uniforms]]. Several other areas of Japan, such as Ibaraki and Chiba, are planning something similar. Whether a longer [[weekend|noon|midnight|textbook]] really leads to more babies, nobody yet knows.

## 加州讓學生晚點上課
California is the first state in the United States to tell schools to start later. Since the 2022–2023 school year, its middle schools may not begin before 8 a.m. and its high schools not before 8:30 a.m., on every [[weekday|weekend|midnight|noon]]. The law was signed in 2019, and districts were given three years to change their timetables; schools in the countryside may ask to be left out. Doctors had argued for this for years. During the teenage years, the body clock moves later, so many students cannot fall asleep until nearly [[midnight|noon|weekday|weekend]], no matter how early they go to bed. An early bell then costs them an hour of [[sleep|garbage|traffic|weather]] every single day. A study published in 2026 found that students in California now sleep longer, report better mental health, and even get slightly higher [[scores|midnights|noons|weekends]] in math and English. Not everyone is happy. Some parents leave for work before their children leave for school, and sports practice has moved later. But students say they no longer need to sleep until [[noon|midnight|weekday|weekend]] on Saturday just to catch up.
> 1 on every weekday「每個上課日」。weekend（週末）不上學，midnight、noon 也不是日子。
> 2 cannot fall asleep until nearly midnight「要到接近午夜才睡得著」。noon（正午）不是睡覺時間，weekday、weekend 是日子，不是時刻。
> 3 costs them an hour of sleep「讓他們少睡一小時」。garbage、traffic、weather 都不是可以被「花掉」的東西。
> 4 get slightly higher scores「成績稍微進步」。midnights、noons、weekends 都不是可以變高的東西。
> 5 sleep until noon on Saturday「星期六睡到中午」。midnight 是半夜，weekday、weekend 是日子，都不能接在 until 後面表示這個時刻。
+ state = 州
+ district = 學區
+ timetable = 作息表
+ body clock = 生理時鐘
+ mental health = 心理健康
= 加州是美國第一個要求學校延後上課的州。從 2022 到 2023 學年起，州內的國中第一節不得早於八點，高中不得早於八點半，每個上課日都是如此。這條法律在 2019 年簽署，給各學區三年時間調整作息；鄉下的學校可以申請排除適用。醫師為此主張了好幾年。進入青春期後，生理時鐘會往後移，所以許多學生不管多早上床，都要到接近午夜才睡得著。太早的鐘聲於是讓他們每天少睡一小時。2026 年發表的一項研究發現，加州學生現在睡得比較久、心理健康狀況較好，數學和英文成績甚至略有進步。不是每個人都滿意。有些父母上班的時間比孩子上學還早，運動練習也往後延。但學生說，他們再也不必為了補眠而在星期六睡到中午。

## 台灣的高中生八點十分到校

On the other four [[weekdays|weekends|midnights|noons]], students only have to arrive by 8:10 and may use the time before the first class as they like. Tests are not allowed in that period, nor in the eighth class after school; since 2024 the same rules cover junior high schools too. Before the change, many students reached school by 7:30, after going to bed close to [[midnight|noon|weekday|weekend]]. In 2020, more than ten thousand people [[agreed|baked|borrowed|prayed]] with an online proposal asking for school to begin at 9:30; the Ministry of Education decided that this was too big a jump and chose 8:10 instead. Some teachers [[worry|bake|borrow|pray]] that students will simply stay up even later, or sleep away the whole [[weekend|noon|midnight|uniform]]. Our class is divided too. Half of us use the extra half hour for sleep, and half of us use it to finish homework we did not do the night before.

> 2 going to bed close to midnight「接近午夜才上床」。noon 是中午，weekday、weekend 是日子，都不能說「接近」某個就寢時刻。

> 5 sleep away the whole weekend「把整個週末睡掉」。noon、midnight、uniform 都不能用 whole 加上「睡掉」。

## 101 的午夜煙火

The tower itself becomes a clock: floor by floor, the lights count down the last [[minutes|midnights|noons|weekends]] of the year, and the crowd counts with them. Then, exactly at [[midnight|noon|weekday|weekend]], fireworks shoot out of the sides of the building, level after level, while music plays from the square. The show that welcomed 2025 lasted six minutes and was built around Taiwan's baseball [[team|garbage|traffic|weather]], which had won the Premier12 championship in Tokyo a few weeks earlier. More than 222,000 people joined that countdown, online and on the spot. The foreign ministry sends the pictures to television stations around the world, so the tower has become one of the pictures people abroad connect with Taiwan. If you cannot go in person, the easiest plan is a warm room, a screen and some snacks. So ask your family what they are doing [[tonight|noon|midnight|weekday]] — if it happens to be December 31, you already know the answer.

## 歐洲為什麼還在調時鐘
Twice a year, most countries in Europe move their clocks one hour: forward in spring, back in autumn. The change happens in the small hours after [[midnight|noon|weekday|weekend]] on a Sunday, so that fewer people are at work when an hour appears or disappears. Many Europeans hate it. In a survey that collected 4.6 million answers, 84 percent wanted the practice to stop, and in March 2019 the European Parliament voted 410 to 192 to end it; the last change was supposed to come in 2021. It never did. The reason is simple: the countries cannot [[agree|bake|borrow|pray]] on what to do instead. Should everyone keep summer time forever, or winter time forever? Germany mostly wants the changes gone, Italy mostly wants them kept. Time zones are political as well as scientific: Spain sits far to the west but keeps the same clock as Berlin, which is why lunch there often starts long after [[noon|midnight|weekday|weekend]]. Meanwhile the clocks still move on one [[weekend|noon|midnight|textbook]] in March and one in October, and every spring a few million people arrive at work an hour late, having lost an hour of [[sleep|garbage|traffic|weather]].
> 1 in the small hours after midnight「午夜過後的凌晨」，調時鐘的時間。noon 是中午，weekday、weekend 是日子。
> 2 cannot agree on what to do instead「無法就替代做法達成一致」。bake、borrow、pray 都接不上 on what to do。
> 3 lunch there often starts long after noon「那裡的午餐常常遠在中午之後才開始」。midnight、weekday、weekend 都不能用來說午餐時間。
> 4 on one weekend in March and one in October「在三月的一個週末和十月的一個週末」。noon、midnight、textbook 都不能用 one 加月份這樣數。
> 5 having lost an hour of sleep「少睡了一小時」。garbage、traffic、weather 都不是會「失去一小時」的東西。
+ survey = 調查
+ practice = 習慣做法
+ European Parliament = 歐洲議會
+ summer time = 夏令時間
+ time zone = 時區
= 每年兩次，歐洲大多數國家把時鐘調一小時：春天往前，秋天往後。這個改變發生在星期日午夜過後的凌晨，好讓少一點人在那個「多出來」或「消失」的小時裡工作。許多歐洲人討厭它。一項收到四百六十萬份回覆的調查中，百分之八十四的人希望停止這種做法；2019 年 3 月，歐洲議會以 410 票對 192 票通過終止，最後一次調整原本應該在 2021 年。但它從沒發生。原因很簡單：各國無法就替代做法達成一致。要永遠用夏令時間，還是永遠用冬令時間？德國人多半希望取消，義大利人多半希望保留。時區既是科學問題也是政治問題：西班牙位置偏西，卻和柏林用同一個時鐘，所以那裡的午餐常常遠在中午之後才開始。同時，時鐘仍然在三月的一個週末和十月的一個週末被調動，而每年春天都有好幾百萬人少睡一小時、上班遲到一小時。

## 一週為什麼是七天
Why does a week have seven days instead of six or ten? The answer is more than four thousand years old. The Babylonians watched the sky carefully and found seven lights that moved against the stars: the sun, the [[moon|garbage|traffic|weather]], and the five planets they could see. Seven seemed like a complete set, so each [[weekday|weekend|noon|midnight]] was given to one of them. You can still hear it in English and in many other languages: Sunday belongs to the sun, Monday to the moon, Saturday to Saturn. The [[weekend|weekday|noon|midnight]], however, is a much younger idea. For most of history people worked six days and rested on one, if they rested at all. Only about a hundred years ago did factories in Britain and America [[begin|bake|borrow|pray]] to close on Saturday afternoon as well, partly so that workers of different religions could each keep their own holy day. So the seven-day week is ancient and the two-day break is modern — and a few companies are now testing three days off, which means the [[weekend|weekday|noon|midnight]] is still being invented.
> 1 the sun, the moon, and the five planets「太陽、月亮和五顆行星」。garbage、traffic、weather 都不是天上會移動的光點。
> 2 each weekday was given to one of them「每一天分配給其中一個」。weekend、noon、midnight 都不是「一週中的一天」。
> 3 The weekend, however, is a much younger idea「週末是比較晚出現的概念」。weekday、noon、midnight 都不是「兩天的休假」。
> 4 factories began to close on Saturday afternoon「工廠開始在週六下午停工」。bake、borrow、pray 都接不上 to close。
> 5 the weekend is still being invented「週末還在被發明」。weekday、noon、midnight 都不是正在改變的休假制度。
+ Babylonian = 巴比倫人
+ planet = 行星
+ complete = 完整的
+ religion = 宗教
+ holy day = 聖日
= 為什麼一週是七天，而不是六天或十天？答案有四千多年歷史。巴比倫人仔細觀察天空，發現有七個光點會相對於恆星移動：太陽、月亮，以及他們看得見的五顆行星。七看起來像一組完整的數字，於是一週裡的每一天都分配給其中一個。你在英文和許多其他語言裡還聽得出來：Sunday 屬於太陽，Monday 屬於月亮，Saturday 屬於土星。不過，週末是晚得多的概念。歷史上大部分時間，人們工作六天、休息一天，如果真有休息的話。大約只在一百年前，英國和美國的工廠才開始連週六下午也停工，部分原因是讓不同宗教的工人都能守自己的聖日。所以七天一週是古老的，兩天休假是現代的——而現在有些公司正在試三天休假，意思是週末還在被發明。

## 午夜的太陽
North of the Arctic Circle, the sun does not set in summer. At [[midnight|noon|weekday|weekend]] it is still above the sea, only low and orange, as if it could not decide whether to go down. People call it the midnight sun. In Tromsø, in northern Norway, it lasts about two months. Children play outside at [[midnight|noon|weekday|weekend]], and their parents hang thick curtains at the windows so that the family can [[sleep|bake|borrow|pray]]. Visitors say the strangest part is that you stop feeling hungry at the right times. In winter the opposite happens. For weeks the sun never rises at all, and even at [[noon|midnight|weekday|weekend]] the sky is only a blue-grey glow above the mountains. People then use bright lamps at breakfast so that they feel less [[tired|salty|crowded|windy]], and the city holds a half marathon in January, in the dark, with head torches. The same place, half a year apart, can have no night at all and then no day at all.
> 1 At midnight it is still above the sea「午夜時太陽仍在海面上」。noon 是中午，weekday、weekend 是日子，都不能說「仍在海面上」。
> 2 play outside at midnight「在午夜到外面玩」，因為天還亮著。noon、weekday、weekend 都和「天亮到半夜」這個奇景無關。
> 3 so that the family can sleep「好讓家人睡得著」，所以要掛厚窗簾。bake、borrow、pray 都不是厚窗簾的用途。
> 4 even at noon the sky is only a blue-grey glow「連中午天空也只是一片藍灰色的微光」。midnight、weekday、weekend 都不能用來說冬天最亮的那個時刻。
> 5 so that they feel less tired「好讓他們不那麼疲倦」。salty、crowded、windy 都不能形容人的身體感覺。
+ Arctic Circle = 北極圈
+ curtain = 窗簾
+ glow = 微光
+ head torch = 頭燈
+ half marathon = 半程馬拉松
= 在北極圈以北，夏天的太陽不會落下。午夜時它仍在海面上，只是又低又橘，好像決定不了要不要下去。人們叫它午夜太陽。在挪威北部的特羅姆瑟，這種情形持續大約兩個月。小孩在午夜到外面玩，父母則在窗戶掛上厚窗簾，好讓全家睡得著。遊客說最奇怪的是，你會在該餓的時候不覺得餓。冬天則完全相反。好幾週太陽根本不升起，連中午天空也只是山上一片藍灰色的微光。人們於是在早餐時開強光燈，好讓自己不那麼疲倦，而這座城市一月還會在黑暗中辦半程馬拉松，大家戴著頭燈跑。同一個地方，相隔半年，可以完全沒有夜晚，接著完全沒有白天。

## 正午最短的影子
Stand outside at [[noon|midnight|weekday|weekend]] and look down at your shadow: it is the shortest it will be all day. The sun is then at its highest [[point|garbage|traffic|weather]] in the sky, so the light falls on you from almost straight above. Long before clocks existed, people used this. A stick in the ground and a few marks around it make a sundial: when the shadow reached the middle line, it was [[noon|midnight|weekday|weekend]], and everybody in the town knew it. The trouble is that a sundial tells you nothing at [[midnight|noon|weekday|weekend]], and nothing on a cloudy day. In Chiayi, which lies almost exactly on the Tropic of Cancer, something else happens around June 21: the noon sun stands directly overhead, a straight pole looks [[strange|salty|crowded|windy]] with no shadow at all, and the place has built a monument to mark the line. If you want to try the simplest science experiment in the world, push a pencil into the soil and draw around its shadow every hour.
> 1 Stand outside at noon「正午時站到外面」，影子最短的時刻。midnight 是半夜沒有影子，weekday、weekend 是日子。
> 2 at its highest point in the sky「在天空最高點」。garbage、traffic、weather 都不是太陽會到達的位置。
> 3 when the shadow reached the middle line, it was noon「影子到中線時就是正午」。midnight、weekday、weekend 都不是日晷指出的那一刻。
> 4 tells you nothing at midnight「半夜什麼也看不出來」，因為沒有陽光。noon 正是日晷最準的時候，weekday、weekend 是日子。
> 5 a straight pole looks strange with no shadow at all「直立的竿子完全沒有影子，看起來很奇怪」。salty、crowded、windy 都不能形容竿子。
+ shadow = 影子
+ sundial = 日晷
+ Tropic of Cancer = 北回歸線
+ overhead = 在頭頂上
+ monument = 紀念碑
= 正午時站到外面，低頭看看你的影子：這是一天之中最短的時候。太陽此時在天空最高點，光幾乎從正上方照到你身上。在時鐘出現很久以前，人們就利用了這一點。地上插一根棍子、周圍畫幾道刻度，就是一座日晷：影子到中線時就是正午，全鎮的人都知道。麻煩在於日晷在半夜什麼也看不出來，陰天也一樣。嘉義幾乎正好位在北回歸線上，每年六月二十一日前後還會發生另一件事：正午的太陽就在頭頂正上方，直立的竿子完全沒有影子，看起來很奇怪，當地還蓋了一座紀念碑標出這條線。如果你想做世界上最簡單的科學實驗，就把一枝鉛筆插進土裡，每小時沿著影子畫一次。

## 青少年的生理時鐘
If your parents tell you that you are lazy because you cannot fall asleep before [[midnight|noon|weekday|weekend]], science is on your side. During the teenage years the body clock shifts later by one or two hours. The brain starts making its sleep chemical later in the evening, so a fourteen-year-old who lies down at ten may really be wide awake at [[midnight|noon|weekday|weekend]] and half asleep in the first class. Doctors in the United States have recommended since 2014 that schools begin no earlier than 8:30, and they say teenagers need eight to ten hours of [[sleep|garbage|traffic|weather]] a night. Sleeping until [[noon|midnight|weekday|weekend]] on Saturday does not fully pay back the debt, and it pushes the clock even later for Monday. The advice is boring but it works: keep roughly the same hours on a [[weekday|weekend|noon|midnight]] and at the weekend, get sunlight in the morning, and leave the phone in another room. The light from a screen is exactly the signal that tells your brain the night has not started yet.
> 1 cannot fall asleep before midnight「午夜之前睡不著」。noon 是中午，weekday、weekend 是日子，都不是入睡的時刻。
> 2 wide awake at midnight「午夜還很清醒」。noon、weekday、weekend 都不能說一個人「在那時很清醒」而對比第一節課。
> 3 need eight to ten hours of sleep「需要八到十小時的睡眠」。garbage、traffic、weather 都不能用小時計算。
> 4 Sleeping until noon on Saturday「星期六睡到中午」。midnight 是半夜，weekday、weekend 是日子。
> 5 keep roughly the same hours on a weekday and at the weekend「平日和週末作息差不多」。weekend 已在句尾出現，noon、midnight 不是日子。
+ shift = 移動
+ chemical = 化學物質
+ recommend = 建議
+ debt = 債
+ signal = 訊號
= 如果你的父母說你懶，因為你午夜之前睡不著，科學站在你這邊。進入青春期後，生理時鐘會往後移一到兩小時。大腦在傍晚較晚才開始製造睡眠用的化學物質，所以一個十點躺下的十四歲孩子，可能到午夜還很清醒，第一節課卻半睡半醒。美國醫師從 2014 年起就建議學校不要早於八點半開始，並說青少年每晚需要八到十小時的睡眠。星期六睡到中午並不能把睡眠債完全還清，反而把生理時鐘再往後推，影響星期一。建議很無聊，但有效：平日和週末的作息盡量相同、早上曬到陽光、把手機放在別的房間。螢幕的光，正好就是告訴大腦「夜晚還沒開始」的訊號。

## 半夜才出門的動物
Many animals are awake only when you are asleep. Walk through a park at [[midnight|noon|weekday|weekend]] and you will hear more than you expect: insects, frogs, and something moving in the trees. Taiwan's flying squirrels leave their holes after sunset, and the fruit bats of southern Taiwan hang quietly all day and fly out at [[midnight|noon|weekday|weekend]] to look for figs. Night animals share a few tools. Their eyes are large, and a shiny layer behind the eye works like a [[mirror|garbage|traffic|weather]], sending light back through it a second time; that is why a torch makes them glow. Owls have another trick: soft feathers that make almost no [[noise|midnight|noon|weekend]], so a mouse never hears them coming. Scientists say the main reason for a night life is not the dark but the quiet. Fewer animals are hunting, and in hot places the air is cooler. If you want to meet them, go out [[tonight|noon|midnight|weekday]] with a red light instead of a white one, sit down, and wait ten minutes without talking. It is harder than it sounds.
> 1 Walk through a park at midnight「午夜走過公園」。noon 是中午，weekday、weekend 是日子，都不是這段在講的時刻。
> 2 fly out at midnight to look for figs「半夜飛出去找無花果」。noon、weekday、weekend 都不合夜行動物的作息。
> 3 works like a mirror「像鏡子一樣」，把光反射回去。garbage、traffic、weather 都不會反射光。
> 4 make almost no noise「幾乎不發出聲音」。midnight、noon、weekend 都不是羽毛會發出的東西。
> 5 go out tonight with a red light「今晚帶著紅光出門」。noon、midnight、weekday 都不能這樣直接接在 go out 後面。
+ flying squirrel = 飛鼠
+ fruit bat = 果蝠
+ fig = 無花果
+ torch = 手電筒
+ feather = 羽毛
= 許多動物只在你睡覺的時候醒著。午夜走過公園，你會聽到比預期更多的聲音：昆蟲、青蛙，還有樹上某個在動的東西。台灣的飛鼠日落後才離開樹洞，南部的果蝠則整天安靜地倒掛，半夜才飛出去找無花果。夜行動物共用幾種工具。牠們的眼睛很大，眼球後面有一層會反光的構造，像鏡子一樣把光再送回去一次，所以手電筒一照牠們就發亮。貓頭鷹還有另一招：柔軟的羽毛幾乎不發出聲音，老鼠永遠聽不到牠飛來。科學家說，過夜生活的主要原因不是黑暗，而是安靜：捕食的動物比較少，而在炎熱的地方，空氣也比較涼。如果你想遇見牠們，今晚帶一支紅光手電筒出門，坐下來，安靜等十分鐘。這比聽起來困難。

## 時區與換日線
When it is [[noon|midnight|weekday|weekend]] in Taipei, it is four in the morning in London and eleven at night in New York — on the day before. The Earth turns, so the sun is overhead somewhere at every moment, and before trains and telegraphs each town simply set its own clock by the sun. That stopped working as soon as people needed a timetable, so the world was cut into time zones, each roughly one hour wide. The International Date Line runs down the Pacific. Fly east across it and you arrive on the day you have already lived; fly west and you lose a whole [[weekday|weekend|noon|midnight]] from your life. In 1995 Kiribati moved the line around its islands so that the whole country could share one date, because half of it had been a day behind the other half. A few places keep half-hour or forty-five-minute differences, which makes planning a flight [[difficult|salty|crowded|windy]]. So when you message a cousin who studies abroad, remember that your evening may be their breakfast, and that a cheerful "good morning" can arrive at [[midnight|noon|weekday|weekend]]. So check the difference before you call: nobody enjoys a phone ringing at that [[moment|garbage|traffic|weather]].
> 1 When it is noon in Taipei「當台北是中午」，後面對照倫敦清晨、紐約深夜。midnight、weekday、weekend 都不能和那些時刻對照。
> 2 you lose a whole weekday from your life「你的生命少掉一整天」。weekend 是兩天，noon、midnight 是時刻。
> 3 makes planning a flight difficult「讓安排班機變困難」。salty、crowded、windy 都不能形容一件事難不難。
> 4 can arrive at midnight「可能在半夜抵達」，因為時差。noon、weekday、weekend 都不是訊息抵達對方的那個時刻。
> 5 a phone ringing at that moment「在那個時刻響起的電話」。garbage、traffic、weather 都不能用 at that 加上「時刻」來指時間點。
+ telegraph = 電報
+ time zone = 時區
+ International Date Line = 國際換日線
+ Kiribati = 吉里巴斯
+ cheerful = 開朗的
= 當台北是中午，倫敦是清晨四點，紐約是前一天的晚上十一點。地球在轉，所以每一刻都有某個地方太陽當頭；在火車和電報出現以前，每個城鎮就各自依太陽調自己的時鐘。但人們一需要時刻表，這套做法就行不通了，於是世界被切成時區，每個大約一小時寬。國際換日線穿過太平洋。往東飛過它，你會抵達已經過完的那一天；往西飛，你的生命就少掉一整天。1995 年，吉里巴斯把這條線繞過自己的島嶼，好讓全國共用同一個日期，因為原本有一半國土比另一半慢一天。有些地方還維持半小時或四十五分鐘的時差，讓安排班機變得困難。所以當你傳訊息給在國外念書的表親時，記得你的傍晚可能是他們的早餐，而一句開朗的「早安」，可能在半夜抵達。所以打電話前先確認時差：沒有人喜歡在那個時刻響起的電話。

## 週末運動員也算
In 2023 a large study followed nearly ninety thousand adults who had worn movement sensors for a week. Scientists wanted to know whether it matters when you exercise. People who reached the recommended 150 minutes a week, but did most of it on only one or two days, are called "[[weekend|weekday|noon|midnight]] warriors". Compared with people who hardly moved, their risk of heart attack and stroke was clearly lower — and it was just as low as for people who exercised a little on every [[weekday|weekend|noon|midnight]]. About 42 percent of the adults in the study turned out to be weekend warriors, and about 34 percent did almost no [[exercise|garbage|traffic|weather]] at all. The lead researcher said the useful part of the message is simple: if your [[weekdays|weekends|noons|midnights]] are full, be active on the days you can. He also warned that the study only shows a link, not a cause, and that starting suddenly with three hard hours is a good way to hurt a knee. Still, for a student with cram school four nights a week, it is good news that two hours of basketball on Saturday morning really [[counts|bakes|borrows|prays]].
> 1 "weekend warriors"「週末戰士」，把一週運動量集中在一兩天的人。weekday、noon、midnight 都不是這個說法。
> 2 exercised a little on every weekday「每個平日都運動一點」，和集中在週末對比。weekend 是週末，noon、midnight 是時刻。
> 3 did almost no exercise at all「幾乎完全不運動」。garbage、traffic、weather 都不是人會做的事。
> 4 if your weekdays are full「如果你平日很滿」。weekends、noons、midnights 都不是上課上班的日子。
> 5 two hours of basketball on Saturday morning really counts「星期六早上兩小時籃球真的算」。bakes、borrows、prays 都接不上這個意思。
+ sensor = 感測器
+ recommend = 建議
+ stroke = 中風
+ warrior = 戰士
+ cram school = 補習班
= 2023 年一項大型研究追蹤了將近九萬名曾戴著運動感測器一週的成年人。科學家想知道，什麼時候運動有沒有差別。那些達到建議的每週 150 分鐘、但大部分集中在一兩天完成的人，被稱為「週末戰士」。和幾乎不動的人相比，他們心臟病和中風的風險明顯較低，而且和每個平日都運動一點的人一樣低。研究中約有百分之四十二的成年人屬於週末戰士，約百分之三十四幾乎完全不運動。主持研究的學者說，這個訊息有用的部分很簡單：如果你平日很滿，就在能動的日子動。他也提醒，研究只顯示關聯，不代表因果，而且突然一次做三小時劇烈運動，是弄傷膝蓋的好方法。不過，對一個一週補習四晚的學生來說，星期六早上兩小時籃球真的算，這是好消息。

## 午夜太陽下的馬拉松
Tromsø, a Norwegian city about 350 kilometres north of the Arctic Circle, holds a marathon that starts at half past eight in the evening. Runners cross the bridge, follow the water, and finish after [[midnight|noon|weekday|weekend]] in full daylight, because in June the sun there never sets. The race began in 1990 and now draws more than six thousand runners from over sixty countries, always on the third [[weekend|weekday|noon|midnight]] of June, around midsummer. It is the northernmost certified marathon in the world. The same organizers hold a half marathon in January, in the polar night, so one small city offers both the brightest [[midnight|noon|weekday|weekend]] and the darkest noon in Europe. Runners say the hard part is not the hills but the [[feeling|garbage|traffic|weather]] that your body should be in bed. Many of them cannot [[sleep|bake|borrow|pray]] afterwards either: it is two in the morning, the sky looks like five in the afternoon, and everybody is sitting outside eating. The race director once admitted that in most years the clouds hide the midnight sun completely.
> 1 finish after midnight in full daylight「午夜之後在大白天裡抵達終點」。noon、weekday、weekend 都不是比賽結束的時刻。
> 2 on the third weekend of June「六月的第三個週末」。weekday、noon、midnight 都不能用「第三個」加月份這樣數。
> 3 the brightest midnight and the darkest noon「最亮的午夜和最暗的中午」。noon 已在句尾出現，weekday、weekend 是日子。
> 4 the feeling that your body should be in bed「身體覺得自己該在床上的那種感覺」。garbage、traffic、weather 都不是這種主觀感受。
> 5 cannot sleep afterwards「跑完後睡不著」。bake、borrow、pray 都接不上這個句子。
+ Arctic Circle = 北極圈
+ certified = 認證的
+ polar night = 永夜
+ midsummer = 仲夏
+ race director = 賽事總監
= 特羅姆瑟是挪威一座位在北極圈以北約三百五十公里的城市，這裡的馬拉松在晚上八點半起跑。跑者越過大橋、沿著海岸，在午夜之後於大白天裡抵達終點，因為六月那裡的太陽從不落下。這場比賽始於 1990 年，現在吸引來自六十多個國家、超過六千名跑者，固定在六月的第三個週末、仲夏前後舉行，是世界上最北的認證馬拉松。同一個主辦單位一月還會在永夜中舉辦半程馬拉松，所以這座小城同時擁有歐洲最亮的午夜和最暗的中午。跑者說，難的不是坡，而是身體覺得自己該在床上的那種感覺。許多人跑完也睡不著：凌晨兩點，天空看起來像下午五點，而所有人都坐在外面吃東西。賽事總監曾經承認，大多數年份雲層其實把午夜太陽完全遮住了。

## 全明星週末

Friday belongs to the young players, Saturday to the skills contests — the three-point shoot-out and the slam-dunk competition — and Sunday to the All-Star game itself, in which nobody plays much defence and everybody smiles. Fans vote for the starting players, so the voting turns into a [[race|garbage|traffic|weather]] of its own, with teams asking people online to click every day. For students in Taiwan the timing is awkward: because of the time difference, the games arrive in the middle of a [[weekday|weekend|noon|midnight]] morning, which is why so many highlights are watched under a desk. Players sometimes complain that the three days are tiring in a strange way: travel, cameras, interviews and almost no [[rest|garbage|traffic|weather]]. By Sunday [[midnight|noon|weekday|weekend]] it is over, everyone flies back, and the real season starts again on Tuesday as if nothing had happened.

## 英國週六下午的電視禁令
In Britain you cannot watch a football match on live television between a quarter to three and a quarter past five on a Saturday. The rule is older than the Premier League. In the 1960s the chairman of Burnley persuaded the other clubs that live games would keep supporters at home, so the [[weekend|weekday|noon|midnight]] afternoon was closed to the cameras. Early kick-offs are allowed, so a match that starts around [[noon|midnight|weekday|weekend]] can be shown, and so can an evening game; only the traditional three o'clock slot is dark. Radio is permitted, which is why millions of people still listen to the results coming in instead of watching them. Britain is the only country with such a rule: a fan in Taipei can watch a three o'clock match that a fan in Manchester can only [[hear|bake|borrow|pray]]. Smaller clubs say the rule protects their [[ticket|garbage|traffic|weather]] sales, since their own games kick off at the same hour. Younger supporters call it old-fashioned and say it only pushes people towards illegal streams. The league keeps it anyway, and three o'clock stays the heart of the English football [[weekend|weekday|noon|midnight]].
> 1 the weekend afternoon was closed to the cameras「週末下午對攝影機關門」。weekday、noon、midnight 都不是這條規定針對的時段。
> 2 a match that starts around noon can be shown「中午左右開打的比賽可以播」。midnight、weekday、weekend 都不是開賽時刻。
> 3 a fan in Manchester can only hear「曼徹斯特的球迷只能聽」，因為電視不能播、廣播可以。bake、borrow、pray 都接不上。
> 4 protects their ticket sales「保護他們的門票收入」。garbage、traffic、weather 都不是小球隊靠的收入。
> 5 three o'clock stays the heart of the English football weekend「三點仍是英格蘭足球週末的核心」。weekday、noon、midnight 都不合。

+ persuade = 說服

## 夜班的代價

What it does mean is that night work needs to be [[planned|baked|borrowed|prayed]] properly: enough days off, bright light during the shift, dark and quiet afterwards, and regular health checks. So the next time you buy a hot drink at [[midnight|noon|weekday|weekend]], remember that the person at the counter is paying for your convenience with their own body clock.

> 3 ruins sleep「破壞睡眠」。garbage、traffic、weather 都不是生理時鐘被打亂後受害的東西。
> 4 night work needs to be planned properly「夜班需要好好規劃」。baked、borrowed、prayed 都不是對工作安排會做的事。
> 5 buy a hot drink at midnight「在半夜買一杯熱飲」。noon、weekday、weekend 都不是便利商店夜班的時刻。

## 兩個週末的音樂節

They had already made history there in 2019 as the first K-pop girl group to perform at the festival at all. A desert afternoon is burning hot and a desert [[midnight|noon|weekday|weekend]] is cold, so the same people wear sun hats at four and blankets at eleven. ## 一首歌為什麼選星期五
In 2020 a Korean group released their first song entirely in English, and it went straight to number one in the United States. Part of that was the music, and part of it was arithmetic. The American charts count a week from Friday to Thursday, so a song that appears on a Friday collects seven full days of streaming and sales in its first week. Appearing on a Monday would throw away the [[weekend|weekday|noon|midnight]], which is exactly when most people listen. Korean companies worked this out, and now a big release usually comes at one in the afternoon Korean time, because that is [[midnight|noon|weekday|weekend]] on the American east coast, where the chart week begins. For fans in Taiwan the sums are easier: Korea is one hour ahead of us, so one in the afternoon there is [[noon|midnight|weekday|weekend]] here, and a few of my classmates really do set an alarm for it. All of this means that a song's first week depends partly on which [[weekday|weekend|noon|midnight]] it is born on. It is a strange thing to learn from pop music, but it is a good lesson in how a [[list|garbage|traffic|weather]] decides what looks popular.
> 1 would throw away the weekend「會白白丟掉週末」，而週末正是大家最常聽歌的時候。weekday、noon、midnight 都不是「大家最常聽歌」的那兩天。
> 2 that is midnight on the American east coast「那是美國東岸的午夜」。noon、weekday、weekend 都不是和首爾下午一點對應的時刻。
> 3 one in the afternoon there is noon here「那裡的下午一點就是這裡的中午」，因為台灣慢一小時。midnight、weekday、weekend 都算不出這個時差。
> 4 which weekday it is born on「它在星期幾誕生」。weekend、noon、midnight 都不是「星期幾」。
> 5 how a list decides what looks popular「一張榜單如何決定什麼看起來紅」。garbage、traffic、weather 都不會決定流行。
+ entirely = 完全地
+ arithmetic = 算術
+ throw away = 白白丟掉
+ sum = 計算
+ alarm = 鬧鐘
= 2020 年，一個韓國團體推出了他們第一首全英文歌，直接登上美國排行榜第一。一部分原因是音樂，一部分原因是算術。美國排行榜以星期五到星期四計算一週，所以在星期五推出的歌，第一週可以收滿七天的串流和銷量。星期一推出就會白白丟掉週末，而週末正是大家最常聽歌的時候。韓國公司算出了這一點，現在大型發行通常選在韓國時間下午一點，因為那正是美國東岸的午夜，也就是排行榜一週的起點。對台灣粉絲來說計算更簡單：韓國比我們快一小時，所以那裡的下午一點就是這裡的中午，而我有幾個同學真的會為此設鬧鐘。這一切意味著，一首歌的首週成績，有一部分取決於它在星期幾誕生。從流行音樂學到這件事有點奇怪，但這是理解「一張榜單如何決定什麼看起來紅」的好教材。

## 關於星期的笑話
My father collects jokes about the days of the week, and he tells them every single week. Here are four. One: why is Monday the worst day of all? Because it is the furthest from the [[weekend|weekday|noon|midnight]]. Two: what did Friday say to Saturday? "I cannot [[wait|bake|borrow|pray]]." Three: the teacher says, "Write one sentence about your [[weekend|weekday|noon|midnight]]." The student writes, "I did nothing, twice." Four: why did the boy bring a pillow to school on Monday morning? Because his [[weekend|weekday|noon|midnight]] had been two days too short. My mother listens to all of this and says they are not jokes, only [[facts|garbage|traffic|weather]]. ## 入夜之後的那條街
A night market is a street with two jobs. Nothing much happens there at [[noon|midnight|weekday|weekend]]: the same road is a quiet row of metal shutters and parked scooters, and the only sound is a radio from somebody's kitchen. By seven the stalls are up, the lights are on, and the smell of fried chicken, oyster omelettes and stinky tofu reaches the corner before you do. On a [[weekend|weekday|noon|midnight]] you do not really walk through a night market; you drift, pushed along by everybody else. Some stalls stay open past [[midnight|noon|weekday|weekend]], and the last customers are students, nurses coming off a shift and taxi drivers. A night market tells you more about a town than its museum does, because everybody is there: grandparents, babies, couples, and a dog that clearly knows which stall drops the most food. If you have one free evening, ask somebody local which market to visit [[tonight|noon|midnight|weekday]].

> 2 On a weekend you do not really walk「週末你根本走不動」。weekday、noon、midnight 都不是人最多的那兩天。
> 3 stay open past midnight「營業到午夜以後」。noon、weekday、weekend 都不是收攤的時刻。
> 4 the goldfish my mother will never let me keep「媽媽永遠不讓我養的金魚」。bake、borrow、pray 都接不上 let me。
> 5 which market to visit tonight「今晚該去哪個夜市」。noon、midnight、weekday 都不能這樣直接接在 visit 後面當時間。

= 夜市是一條有兩份工作的街。中午那裡沒什麼事：同一條路是一排安靜的鐵門和停著的機車，唯一的聲音是某戶廚房傳來的收音機。到了七點，攤子架起來、燈亮起來，炸雞、蚵仔煎和臭豆腐的味道比你更早到達街角。週末你根本不是走過夜市，而是被所有人推著隨人潮移動。有些攤子營業到午夜以後，最後的客人是學生、剛下班的護理師和計程車司機。我最喜歡的不是食物，而是遊戲：套圈圈、射氣球，還有媽媽永遠不讓我養的金魚。夜市比博物館更能告訴你一個城鎮的事，因為所有人都在那裡：阿公阿嬤、嬰兒、情侶，還有一隻顯然知道哪個攤子掉最多食物的狗。如果你只有一個晚上，問問當地人今晚該去哪個夜市。

## 阿公的二十分鐘
My grandfather has exactly one rule in his house: nobody disturbs him at [[noon|midnight|weekday|weekend]]. After lunch he carries a plastic chair into the shade of the mango tree, lays a towel over his eyes, and sleeps for twenty minutes. Then he gets up, drinks cold tea, and goes back to the field. He is seventy-eight and says the nap is the whole secret. Scientists mostly agree with him: a sleep of about twenty minutes can leave you more [[careful|salty|crowded|windy]] and quicker for several hours afterwards, while an hour often leaves you heavy and slow, because you wake out of deep sleep. On a [[weekday|weekend|noon|midnight]] I am at school, so I only see the ritual on Saturday and Sunday. Last year I tried it myself during the summer holiday. The first time I woke up at four in the afternoon and then could not fall asleep until [[midnight|noon|weekday|weekend]]. Now I set an alarm. My grandfather does not: he says a body that has worked all morning knows when to [[wake|bake|borrow|pray]] up.
> 1 nobody disturbs him at noon「中午沒有人可以打擾他」。midnight、weekday、weekend 都不是午睡的時刻。
> 2 leave you more careful and quicker「讓你更專注、反應更快」。salty、crowded、windy 都不能形容人睡醒後的狀態。
> 3 On a weekday I am at school「平日我在學校」，所以只有週末看得到。weekend 是週末，noon、midnight 是時刻。
> 4 could not fall asleep until midnight「到午夜才睡得著」。noon、weekday、weekend 都不是晚上入睡的時刻。
> 5 knows when to wake up「知道什麼時候該醒」。bake、borrow、pray 都接不上 up 這個字。
+ disturb = 打擾
+ shade = 陰影處
+ nap = 小睡
+ ritual = 固定儀式
+ deep sleep = 深睡
= 我阿公家裡只有一條規矩：中午沒有人可以打擾他。午飯後，他把一張塑膠椅搬到芒果樹的陰影下，用毛巾蓋住眼睛，睡二十分鐘。然後他起身、喝冷茶，回田裡去。他七十八歲，說午睡就是全部的秘訣。科學家大致同意：大約二十分鐘的睡眠可以讓你之後好幾個小時更專注、反應更快；睡一小時則常讓人昏沉遲緩，因為你是從深睡中被叫醒的。平日我在學校，所以只有星期六和星期日看得到這個儀式。去年暑假我自己試了一次。第一次我睡到下午四點才醒，結果到午夜才睡得著。現在我會設鬧鐘。我阿公不設：他說一個整個上午都在工作的身體，知道什麼時候該醒。
