## 四十萬顆天燈之後
Pingxi, in the hills of New Taipei, is famous for sky [[lanterns|Easters|Valentines|Christmases]]. People write a wish on the paper, light the fuel at the bottom, and watch the hot air carry it into the dark; a travel website once named the Pingxi festival the cultural event in Taiwan most worth recommending to foreign visitors. About 400,000 of them are released there every year, and every one of them comes down somewhere. The paper and the wire frames land in forests and rivers, where they can injure wild animals and are extremely hard to collect. The city government now runs an exchange: bring in five sheets of used [[lantern|Easter|Valentine|Christmas]] paper and you get a rubbish bag, ten sheets a pack of tissues, fifty sheets a bottle of washing-up liquid, and local shops will take one sheet as one dollar off your shopping. After the 2025 festival, more than a hundred volunteers walked the mountain paths and brought down over 650 kilograms of [[garbage|Halloween|Valentine|Easter]] in fifty-three bags. A university has even been testing drones with claws to lift [[lanterns|Easters|Valentines|Christmases]] out of trees. Nobody wants to end the tradition; they want the wish without the mess. People in Pingxi put it more simply: keep the [[lanterns|Easters|Valentines|Christmases]], but take the paper back.

## 十二月二十五日為什麼放假

It used to be a public holiday, then it stopped being one in 2001, and then in May 2025 the legislature passed a new law that made it a holiday again, together with the day before Lunar New Year's Eve, Teachers' Day and Retrocession Day. The law took effect at the end of that month, so the first of these new days off arrived in the second half of 2025. In 2026 the twenty-fifth falls on a Friday, which gives everybody a three-day [[holiday|Christmas|Easter|Halloween]]. Shops, of course, do not care what the day is called. Trees go up in November, and by the middle of December every café plays the same twelve songs. So Taiwan has the odd situation of a [[Christmas|Easter|Halloween|Valentine]] that is celebrated everywhere and named after a constitution, and nobody finds this strange — least of all students, who [[enjoy|bake|borrow|pray]] the day off whatever it is called. ## 二〇二五年，東西方同一天過復活節
[[Easter|Christmas|Halloween|Valentine]] does not have a fixed date. It is fixed instead by a rule agreed in the year 325 at a council in Nicaea, in what is now Turkey: the first Sunday after the first full moon on or after the spring equinox. That is why it wanders between late March and late April. There is a complication. Eastern churches work the rule out using the older Julian [[calendar|Christmas|Easter|lantern]], while Western churches use the Gregorian one, so the two [[Easters|Christmases|Halloweens|lanterns]] usually land on different Sundays, sometimes weeks apart. In 2025 they did not. Both fell on 20 April, the first time since 2017, and it happened in the 1,700th year after that council in Nicaea, which several church leaders called an [[opportunity|garbage|traffic|weather]] rather than a coincidence. They have been asking for a common date for decades; so far nothing has changed, partly because the calendar of public holidays in many countries is tied to the Western date. The dates will meet again in 2028, 2031 and 2034. If you want to surprise an adult, ask them why the date of [[Easter|Christmas|Halloween|Valentine]] depends on the moon.

> 2 the older Julian calendar「較舊的儒略曆」。Christmas、Easter、lantern 都不是曆法。
> 3 the two Easters usually land on different Sundays「兩邊的復活節通常落在不同的星期日」。Christmases、Halloweens、lanterns 都不會分成東西兩套日期。
> 4 called it an opportunity rather than a coincidence「稱它是機會，而不是巧合」。garbage、traffic、weather 都接不上這個對比。
> 5 why the date of Easter depends on the moon「為什麼復活節的日期要看月亮」。Christmas、Halloween、Valentine 的日期都和月亮無關。

+ wander = 遊移

+ coincidence = 巧合
= 復活節沒有固定日期。它是由西元 325 年在尼西亞（今土耳其）舉行的會議所訂下的規則決定：春分之後第一個滿月之後的第一個星期日。這就是它會在三月底到四月底之間遊移的原因。其中還有複雜之處。東方教會用較舊的儒略曆推算這條規則，西方教會用格里曆，所以兩邊的復活節通常落在不同的星期日，有時相差好幾週。2025 年卻沒有。兩者都在 4 月 20 日，是 2017 年以來第一次，而且發生在尼西亞會議之後的第一千七百年，好幾位教會領袖稱這是機會，而不是巧合。他們要求共同日期已經數十年；到目前為止沒有改變，部分原因是許多國家的國定假日行事曆綁著西方的日期。兩邊的日期會在 2028、2031 和 2034 年再度重合。如果你想讓大人意外，就問他們為什麼復活節的日期要看月亮。

## 梨泰院的那一夜
On 29 October 2022, about a hundred thousand people went to Itaewon, a lively district in Seoul, for [[Halloween|Easter|Christmas|lantern]]. It was the first one after the pandemic restrictions had been lifted, and most of the crowd were in their twenties. Many of them funnelled into one sloping alley about four metres wide. In a space that narrow a crowd stops behaving like a group of people and starts behaving like a fluid: once the density passes a certain point, nobody can move, nobody can [[breathe|bake|borrow|pray]] properly, and nobody can get out. By the end of the night 159 people had died and almost two hundred were injured. South Korea changed the way it thinks about [[safety|garbage|traffic|weather]] afterwards; crowd experts had been saying for years that such disasters are predictable and preventable, and that blaming the crowd misses the point, because planning, counting and one-way streets are the responsibility of the people in charge. Itaewon has held quiet [[Halloweens|Easters|Christmases|lanterns]] ever since, with a banner that asks for safety instead of decorations. The lesson that students are taught now is simple and worth keeping: if a crowd is pressing you and you cannot choose where your feet go, leave early, before leaving stops being a choice. Our own school added one line to its trip rules after that night: agree on a meeting point before you go in, and never follow a crowd into a narrow street, on [[Halloween|Easter|Christmas|lantern]] night or any other.

## 南瓜燈本來是蕪菁
The grinning pumpkin is the picture everybody has of [[Halloween|Easter|Christmas|Valentine]], and it is a fairly recent invention. The custom came from Ireland and Scotland, where people carved faces into turnips and beets, put a coal or a candle inside, and set the little [[lantern|Easter|Valentine|Christmas]] by the door on the night when, in the old belief, the border between this world and the next was thin. The point was to frighten away whatever might be wandering. When Irish families emigrated to North America in the nineteenth century they found a vegetable that was far easier to carve and much larger: the pumpkin. If you have ever tried to hollow out a turnip, you will understand why the pumpkin [[won|baked|borrowed|prayed]]. The old faces are worth looking up; photographs of nineteenth-century turnip lanterns are considerably more frightening than anything sold in a shop today. Modern [[Halloween|Easter|Christmas|lantern]] costumes, sweets and parties grew mostly in twentieth-century America and then travelled back to Europe and on to Asia, which is why a festival with roots in an old Celtic calendar now arrives in Taiwan as a cram-school party with plastic [[pumpkins|Easters|Valentines|Christmases]].
> 1 the picture everybody has of Halloween「大家對萬聖節的印象」。Easter、Christmas、Valentine 都不是南瓜燈的節日。
> 2 set the little lantern by the door「把那個小燈放在門邊」。Easter、Valentine、Christmas 都不是可以放在門邊的燈。
> 3 why the pumpkin won「為什麼南瓜贏了」。baked、borrowed、prayed 都接不上這個比較。
> 4 Modern Halloween costumes, sweets and parties「現代萬聖節的服裝、糖果和派對」。Easter、Christmas、lantern 都不合。
> 5 a cram-school party with plastic pumpkins「補習班派對加塑膠南瓜」。Easters、Valentines、Christmases 都不是萬聖節的裝飾。

## 天燈為什麼飛得起來
A sky [[lantern|Easter|Valentine|Christmas]] is a physics lesson you can hold in your hands. Inside the paper bag, a small pad of fuel burns and heats the air. Hot air is thinner than cold air — the same number of molecules spread out over more space — so the air inside weighs less than the air it has pushed out of the way, and the whole thing rises. It is exactly how a hot-air balloon works, at one-thousandth of the size. Three things decide whether it [[rises|bakes|borrows|prays]]: the paper must be light and must not leak, the fuel must burn steadily, and the night must not be too windy or too wet. This is also why the festival in Pingxi happens in winter, when the cold air outside makes the difference bigger. When the fuel runs out the air cools, and the [[lantern|Easter|Christmas|Valentine]] comes down — usually several kilometres away, which is the part of the physics that creates the rubbish problem. Our science teacher let us make a small one out of tissue paper and birthday candles in the school yard. It rose about four metres, caught on a basketball hoop, and taught us more about hot air than any [[test|garbage|traffic|weather]] could. If you ever release one, write your wish on it, and then pick up somebody else's [[lantern|Easter|Valentine|Christmas]] on the way home.
> 1 A sky lantern is a physics lesson「天燈是一堂物理課」。Easter、Valentine、Christmas 都不是會飛的東西。
> 2 Three things decide whether it rises「三件事決定它升不升得起來」。bakes、borrows、prays 都接不上 whether it。
> 3 the lantern comes down「天燈就會落下」。Easter、Christmas、Valentine 都不會落下。
> 4 taught us more than any test could「比任何考試都教得更多」。garbage、traffic、weather 都不是學習的方式。
> 5 pick up somebody else's lantern on the way home「回家路上順手撿起別人的天燈」。Easter、Valentine、Christmas 都不是會掉在路上的東西。
+ pad = 墊、塊
+ molecule = 分子
+ hot-air balloon = 熱氣球
+ leak = 漏
+ hoop = 籃框
= 天燈是一堂可以捧在手上的物理課。紙袋裡面有一小塊燃料燃燒，把空氣加熱。熱空氣比冷空氣稀薄——同樣數目的分子散開在更大的空間裡——所以裡面的空氣比它推開的空氣輕，整個東西就上升。這和熱氣球的原理完全一樣，只是小了一千倍。三件事決定它飛不飛得起來：紙必須輕而且不能漏、燃料必須穩定燃燒、當晚不能太大風或太濕。這也是平溪的天燈節在冬天舉行的原因，外面的冷空氣讓溫差更大。燃料燒完後空氣變冷，天燈就落下——通常在好幾公里外，而這正是造成垃圾問題的那一段物理。我們的自然老師讓我們在操場上用薄紙和生日蠟燭做了一個小的。它升高大約四公尺，卡在籃框上，卻比任何考試都更能教我們熱空氣是怎麼回事。如果你有機會放一顆，就把願望寫上去，然後回家路上順手撿起別人的天燈。

## 聖誕樹與那株紅色植物
Two plants turn up everywhere in December. The first is the [[Christmas|Easter|Halloween|Valentine]] tree, which is a fir, a spruce or a pine: trees that keep their needles through winter, which is exactly why they were brought indoors in northern Europe long before anybody called it Christmas. An evergreen in a dark month is a promise. The custom spread from Germany to Britain in the nineteenth century, partly because a royal family was drawn in a magazine standing around one, and from there it went everywhere. The second plant is the poinsettia, which comes from Mexico and is not really red at all: those are leaves, not petals, and they only turn colour when the nights are long enough. Growers therefore keep them in the dark on purpose, because a [[Christmas|Easter|Halloween|Valentine]] plant that is still green in December cannot be [[sold|baked|borrowed|prayed]]. Taiwan grows a great many of them in Nantou and Changhua. There is one more thing worth knowing if you have a cat: the poinsettia is often called poisonous, but most sources now say it causes only mild [[trouble|Christmas|Easter|lantern]], while the real danger at this time of year is chocolate. So both plants are winter engineering: one keeps its leaves against the cold, and the other is tricked into turning red in time for [[Christmas|Easter|Halloween|Valentine]].

> 2 a Christmas plant that is still green in December「十二月還是綠色的聖誕植物」。Easter、Halloween、Valentine 都不是這種植物的用途。
> 3 cannot be sold「賣不出去」。baked、borrowed、prayed 都接不上 cannot be。
> 4 causes only mild trouble「只造成輕微的不適」。Christmas、Easter、lantern 都不是中毒的症狀。
> 5 in time for Christmas「趕在聖誕節之前」。Easter、Halloween、Valentine 都不是聖誕紅要趕上的節日。

+ poinsettia = 聖誕紅
+ petal = 花瓣
= 十二月有兩種植物到處都看得到。第一種是聖誕樹，也就是冷杉、雲杉或松樹：這些樹整個冬天都保留針葉，而這正是它們在還沒有人稱之為聖誕節的年代，就被北歐人搬進屋裡的原因。在最暗的月份裡，一棵常綠樹就是一個承諾。這個習俗在十九世紀從德國傳到英國，部分原因是一本雜誌畫出了王室圍著一棵樹的樣子，從那裡就傳到各地。第二種植物是聖誕紅，它來自墨西哥，而且根本不是真的紅：那些是葉子，不是花瓣，而且只有在夜晚足夠長時才會轉色。所以栽培者會刻意把它們放在暗處，因為十二月還是綠色的聖誕植物賣不出去。台灣在南投和彰化種了非常多。如果你家有貓，還有一件事值得知道：聖誕紅常被說有毒，但現在多數資料認為它只會造成輕微的不適，這個季節真正的危險是巧克力。所以這兩種植物都是冬天的工程：一種靠著留住葉子對抗寒冷，另一種被人騙著在聖誕節前轉紅。

## 巧克力為什麼要融在嘴裡
Every February, shops fill up with chocolate because of one [[Valentine|Easter|Halloween|lantern]] in the middle of the month. The chocolate itself is better science than the marketing suggests. Cocoa butter is unusual among fats: it is solid at room temperature and melts at about 34°C, which is just below the temperature of your mouth. That is why good chocolate snaps when you break it and then disappears on your tongue. Getting it to behave takes work. Makers heat and cool the chocolate through a precise series of temperatures, a process called tempering, so that the fat forms the right kind of crystal; skip it and you get a soft bar with pale streaks. The taste comes earlier, from fermenting and roasting the beans, and the bitterness that adults claim to [[enjoy|bake|borrow|pray]] comes from the same chemicals that make cocoa interesting to study. Cocoa itself grows only near the equator, and in recent years disease and strange weather in West Africa have pushed prices to records, so your [[Valentine|Easter|Halloween|Christmas]] box is also a climate story. Dark chocolate, by the way, is dangerous for dogs for the same reason it is [[popular|salty|crowded|windy]] with people: the chemicals are strong. Give the dog a biscuit instead, and keep the [[Valentine|Easter|Halloween|lantern]] box for yourself.
> 1 because of one Valentine in the middle of the month「因為月中那個情人節」。Easter、Halloween、lantern 都不在二月。
> 2 the bitterness that adults claim to enjoy「大人聲稱喜歡的苦味」。bake、borrow、pray 都接不上 claim to。
> 3 your Valentine box is also a climate story「你的情人節禮盒也是一個氣候故事」。Easter、Halloween、Christmas 都不是二月的禮盒。
> 4 the same reason it is popular with people「它受人類歡迎的同一個原因」。salty、crowded、windy 都不能形容巧克力受不受歡迎。
> 5 keep the Valentine box for yourself「情人節禮盒自己留著」。Easter、Halloween、lantern 都不是二月那個禮盒。
+ cocoa butter = 可可脂
+ snap = 清脆斷裂
+ temper = 調溫
+ crystal = 結晶
+ ferment = 發酵
= 每年二月，店裡堆滿巧克力，因為月中那個情人節。巧克力本身的科學比它的行銷有趣。可可脂在油脂裡很特別：它在室溫下是固體，大約 34°C 融化，剛好低於你口腔的溫度。這就是為什麼好的巧克力折斷時會清脆作響，然後在舌頭上消失。要讓它乖乖聽話需要工夫。製作者會讓巧克力經過一連串精確的升溫與降溫，這個過程叫調溫，好讓油脂形成正確的結晶；省略它，你就會得到一條軟軟、帶著白色條紋的巧克力。味道來得更早，來自可可豆的發酵和烘焙，而大人聲稱喜歡的苦味，來自同一批讓可可值得研究的化學物質。可可只長在赤道附近，而近年西非的病害與異常天氣把價格推上新高，所以你的情人節禮盒也是一個氣候故事。順帶一提，黑巧克力對狗危險的原因，和它受人類歡迎的原因一樣：那些化學物質很強。給狗一塊餅乾就好，情人節禮盒自己留著。

## 為什麼是十二月二十五日
Nobody knows the date of the first [[Christmas|Easter|Halloween|Valentine]], and the Bible does not give one. The 25th of December was chosen in Rome, several hundred years later, and historians argue about why. One explanation is the sun. Around the winter solstice the days stop getting shorter, and Romans already held winter festivals at that time; placing a new feast there made it easy for people to [[change|bake|borrow|pray]] without losing their midwinter party. Another explanation is arithmetic: some early Christians dated the beginning of the story to late March, and nine months after late March is late December. Eastern churches that use a different calendar still celebrate [[Christmas|Easter|Halloween|Valentine]] on 7 January, which is the same day by the older reckoning. The familiar details came later and from everywhere: trees from Germany, cards from Victorian Britain, the red-suited figure largely from American illustrators and advertisements. None of this makes the festival less real. It does mean that when somebody tells you the "true" [[Christmas|Easter|Halloween|Valentine]] is being lost, it is worth asking which century they mean. Our class decided that the best argument for keeping the date in December is that a festival of light in the darkest month is a better [[idea|garbage|traffic|weather]] than one in June.
> 1 the date of the first Christmas「第一個聖誕節的日期」。Easter、Halloween、Valentine 都不是十二月那個節日。
> 2 easy for people to change「讓人們容易改變（信仰習慣）」。bake、borrow、pray 都接不上這個對比。
> 3 still celebrate Christmas on 7 January「仍在 1 月 7 日過聖誕節」。Easter、Halloween、Valentine 都不是那一天在慶祝的節日。
> 4 the "true" Christmas is being lost「「真正的」聖誕節正在流失」。Easter、Halloween、Valentine 都不是這個爭論的主題。
> 5 is a better idea than one in June「比辦在六月是更好的主意」。garbage、traffic、weather 都不能接在 a better 後面。
+ solstice = 至日（冬至）
+ feast = 節慶
+ reckoning = 計算方式
+ illustrator = 插畫家
+ advertisement = 廣告
= 沒有人知道第一個聖誕節的日期，聖經也沒有寫。十二月二十五日是好幾百年後在羅馬選定的，而歷史學家對原因仍有爭論。一種解釋是太陽。冬至前後，白天不再變短，而羅馬人原本就在那時舉行冬季節慶；把新的節日放在那裡，讓人們容易改變，又不必失去自己的仲冬派對。另一種解釋是算術：有些早期基督徒把故事的開始定在三月下旬，而三月下旬之後九個月就是十二月下旬。使用不同曆法的東方教會至今在 1 月 7 日慶祝，那依舊曆算是同一天。熟悉的細節都是後來才加上的，而且來自各地：樹來自德國，卡片來自維多利亞時代的英國，那位穿紅衣的角色主要來自美國插畫家和廣告。這些都不會讓這個節日變得不真實。但它確實意味著，當有人告訴你「真正的」聖誕節正在流失時，值得問一句：你說的是哪個世紀？我們班的結論是，把日期留在十二月最好的理由是——在最暗的月份辦一個光的節慶，比辦在六月合理得多。

## 兔子、蛋，和春天
Why does [[Easter|Christmas|Halloween|lantern]] involve a rabbit that delivers eggs? Neither appears in the story the festival is about. Both come from spring. An egg is the oldest possible symbol of new life, and in the weeks before the festival Christians traditionally did not eat eggs, so by the end there were a great many to use up; boiling and decorating them solved a practical problem and made a pretty one. The rabbit arrived from German folklore, where a hare brought coloured eggs to children who had been good, and German families carried the custom to America in the eighteenth [[century|Christmas|Easter|Valentine]]. Hares are everywhere in spring and famously good at producing families, which is probably enough explanation. Chocolate eggs are a Victorian invention, and in Britain only [[Christmas|Easter|Halloween|Valentine]] sells more chocolate than this festival does. In Taiwan, [[Easter|Christmas|Halloween|Valentine]] is mostly a church event and a hunt organised by English teachers, which is how I first met the word "hunt". My own teacher hid forty plastic eggs around the playground, and we found thirty-eight. The last two turned up in June, which the [[principal|garbage|traffic|weather]] did not find funny.

> 3 only Christmas sells more chocolate「只有聖誕節賣出更多巧克力」。Easter 就是本篇的節日，Halloween、Valentine 的銷量都不是英國的第一名。
> 4 In Taiwan, Easter is mostly a church event「在台灣，復活節主要是教會活動」。Christmas、Halloween、Valentine 在台灣都比復活節熱鬧。
> 5 which the principal did not find funny「校長覺得不好笑」。garbage、traffic、weather 都不會覺得某件事好不好笑。

## 那一年，偶像取消了萬聖節
Korean idols used to post costume photographs every year at the end of October. Companies organised studio shoots, variety shows ran [[Halloween|Easter|Christmas|lantern]] specials, and the costumes were half the fun of the season. In 2022 that stopped. Three days before the date, the crowd disaster in Itaewon killed 159 people, most of them the same age as the fans, and the industry cancelled everything within hours: posts deleted, specials pulled, concerts turned into quiet events with no [[Halloween|Easter|Christmas|Valentine]] decorations at all. Several groups released statements saying they could not celebrate that week. It would be easy to be cynical about this, but the people who died were from exactly that audience, and a few of the dead were themselves trainees or staff in the industry. In the years since, [[Halloween|Easter|Christmas|Valentine]] content has come back slowly and more carefully, with fewer street events and more indoor ones. Fans argue about where the line is. What strikes me is that a festival imported for fun became, in one country, a date that cannot be used lightly — which is also true of certain dates in Taiwan, and is the sort of thing you only [[learn|bake|borrow|pray]] by living somewhere. When our teacher asked us to name a date in Taiwan that cannot be used for a party, the room answered in one [[voice|garbage|traffic|weather]].

> 3 Halloween content has come back slowly「萬聖節的內容慢慢回來」。Easter、Christmas、Valentine 都不是這段在說的節日。
> 4 the sort of thing you only learn by living somewhere「只有住在那裡才會懂的事」。bake、borrow、pray 都接不上這個句型。

## 二月十四日與三月十四日
Korea has turned [[Valentine|Easter|Christmas|lantern]]'s Day into a series. Some fandoms now do something different and donate instead: rice to a food bank, money to an animal shelter, or books to a library, in the group's name, on the [[date|garbage|traffic|weather]] itself. My cousin's fandom sent 300 kilograms of rice. I find that a better use of a [[Valentine|Easter|Christmas|lantern]] than another plastic heart, and so, apparently, do the people who received the rice.

= 三個我叔叔每年春天都要講的笑話，不管有沒有人想聽。第一個：兔子在復活節怎麼旅行？搭「野兔機」（hare-plane，諧音 aeroplane）。第二個：蛋對滾水說了什麼？「我可能要一陣子才會變硬，因為我一分鐘前才從冰箱出來。」第三個：我小表妹問為什麼要彩繪復活節蛋，我叔叔說這樣才找得到，她說那就不該把蛋塗成綠色。她五歲，而且剛剛贏了那場爭論，這件事我叔叔始終沒有原諒。還有第四個，他留到飯後才講：尋蛋活動隔天早上，雞為什麼要過馬路？為了把牠的蛋拿回來。現在沒有人笑了，他說這證明我們對傳統毫無敬意。

## 阿嬤的湯圓
On the afternoon of the [[Lantern|Christmas|Easter|Valentine]] Festival my grandmother makes tangyuan, and the kitchen becomes a small factory with bad management. She mixes glutinous rice flour with warm water until it feels right, which she cannot explain and I cannot copy, and then we roll the dough into balls. Mine are never the same size as hers. The filling is black sesame, and the test of a good one is that it is soft enough to [[escape|bake|borrow|pray]] when you bite it, which it does, usually onto somebody's shirt. We cook them in water until they rise to the top, and that is the whole recipe. The sweet soup is ginger for the adults and plain for the children. While we work she tells the same stories, and I have stopped minding that they are the same. The last thing we do is carry two bowls to the neighbour, who is ninety and does not cook now, and then we eat ours in front of a programme about the [[lanterns|Easters|Christmases|Valentines]] in Taipei. My grandmother is not interested in the big [[lantern|Easter|Christmas|Valentine]] displays on television. She says the festival is round, like the food, and that the point of a [[Lantern|Christmas|Easter|Halloween]] Festival is simply that the year has come back to where it started.
> 1 the afternoon of the Lantern Festival「元宵節的下午」。Christmas、Easter、Valentine 都不是吃湯圓的節日。
> 2 soft enough to escape when you bite it「軟到咬下去就會流出來」。bake、borrow、pray 都接不上 enough to。
> 3 a programme about the lanterns in Taipei「關於台北那些燈的節目」。Easters、Christmases、Valentines 都不是電視上在轉播的東西。
> 4 the big lantern displays on television「電視上的大型燈組」。Easter、Christmas、Valentine 都不是展出的燈。
> 5 the point of a Lantern Festival「元宵節的意義」。Christmas、Easter、Halloween 都不是「一年回到起點」的那個節日。
+ glutinous rice flour = 糯米粉
+ dough = 麵團
+ filling = 內餡
+ sesame = 芝麻
+ ginger = 薑
= 元宵節的下午，我阿嬤會做湯圓，廚房於是變成一間管理不良的小工廠。她把糯米粉和溫水混在一起，揉到「手感對了」為止，那是她無法解釋、我也複製不來的東西，然後我們把麵團搓成球。我搓的從來和她的不一樣大。內餡是黑芝麻，而好湯圓的檢驗標準是：軟到你一咬就流出來——而它確實會，通常流到某個人的衣服上。我們在水裡煮到它們浮上來，整個食譜就是這樣。甜湯給大人加薑，小孩的不加。我們一邊做，她一邊講同樣的故事，而我已經不介意它們都一樣。最後一件事是端兩碗給鄰居，她九十歲，現在不煮飯了；然後我們看著電視上關於台北那些燈的節目吃自己的。我阿嬤對電視上的大型燈組沒有興趣。她說這個節日是圓的，像那個食物一樣，而元宵節的意義很簡單：一年又回到了它開始的地方。
