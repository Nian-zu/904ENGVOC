## 最後一個菠蘿麵包

## 日本的零錢盤
Last spring, my family took a trip to Kyoto. On our first day, I went into a small shop to buy a fan for my teacher. When I tried to hand the money to the [[clerk|pumpkin|throat|belt]], he politely pointed to a small tray on the counter. In Japan, many shops ask you to put your money in the tray [[instead|almost|already|ago]] of giving it directly to the worker. I also noticed that the shop did not take [[credit|noodle|knee|sock]] cards. Only cash! Luckily, Dad had some yen in his wallet. The fan was expensive, but I could [[afford|sneeze|spell|shout]] it with my savings. As I left, the clerk bowed and said, "Arigato." Every [[customer|spoon|lemon|toe]] gets a bow there. > 5 every customer「每一位客人」都會得到店員鞠躬。spoon、lemon、toe 都不能被鞠躬道謝。

+ fan = 扇子
+ tray = 托盤

= 去年春天，我們全家去京都旅行。第一天，我走進一家小店，想買一把扇子送給老師。我要把錢交給店員時，他很有禮貌地指了指櫃台上的小盤子。在日本，很多店會請你把錢放在盤子上，而不是直接交給店員。我還注意到這家店不收信用卡，只收現金！還好爸爸的錢包裡有日圓。扇子很貴，但我用自己的存款買得起。我離開時，店員鞠躬說：「Arigato。」在那裡，每一位客人都會得到一個鞠躬。我很喜歡那個小小的時刻。
