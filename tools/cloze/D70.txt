## 末日之鐘
In 1947 a group of scientists who had helped build the atomic bomb drew a [[clock|calendar|machine|list]] on the cover of their magazine, with its hands close to midnight. Midnight stood for disaster. Every January since then, the group has decided where the hands should be. In January 2025 they moved them to 89 seconds before midnight — the closest the [[clock|calendar|machine|science]] had ever been to it in seventy-eight years. The scientists look at three things: nuclear weapons, a warming planet, and new technology that nobody quite controls. It is not a real [[clock|calendar|century|tomorrow]]; nobody can measure in seconds how close the world is to catastrophe, and some scientists dislike the whole idea. But it is a way of asking a question that is otherwise hard to ask out loud: are we leaving a safer world for [[tomorrow|yesterday|century|calendar]]?

## 再見，閏秒
The Earth is not a very good [[clock|calendar|century|tomorrow]]. It turns a little unevenly, and slightly more slowly than it used to, while atomic clocks tick at a perfectly steady rate. Since 1972, whenever the two have drifted more than 0.9 seconds apart, timekeepers have added one extra second — a leap second — to official time. It has been done twenty-seven times. Computers hate it. A leap second in 2012 knocked several large websites offline, and another in 2017 caused [[trouble|garbage|traffic|weather]] for an internet company that millions of sites depend on. In November 2022, government representatives met near Paris and voted to stop adding leap seconds by 2035. Russia voted against, because it wanted to [[wait|bake|borrow|pray]] until 2040. After 2035 the difference will simply be allowed to grow, and somebody, perhaps in the next [[century|calendar|clock|tomorrow]], will have to decide what to do about a whole leap minute. Either way, the second itself will not change: it is defined by atoms, which means the most accurate [[clock|calendar|century|yesterday]] in the world no longer looks at the sky at all.

## 第一個超過一點五度的年份

## 沉默五年的大鐘

## 十九位小數的鐘
How accurate can a [[clock|calendar|century|tomorrow]] be? In July 2025 a laboratory in the United States announced a new record: an atomic clock built around one single trapped aluminium ion, accurate to nineteen decimal places. Put in plainer words, it would not gain or lose a second over a period longer than the age of the universe. The record came after twenty years of small [[changes|garbage|traffic|weather]] to the laser, the ion trap and the vacuum chamber, and the new version is 41 percent more accurate than the previous best. A [[machine|calendar|century|yesterday]] this accurate can even feel gravity: lift it a few centimetres and it ticks very slightly faster, exactly as Einstein predicted. ## 消失的十天
In 1582, ten days disappeared. People in Rome went to bed on 4 October and woke up on 15 October, because the Pope had changed the [[calendar|clock|century|tomorrow]]. The old Roman [[calendar|clock|century|yesterday]] treated a year as 365.25 days, which is about eleven minutes too long. Over more than a thousand years those eleven minutes had added up to ten whole days, and Easter was slowly drifting away from spring. The new rules kept the leap year but added an exception: a year divisible by 100 is not a leap year unless it is also divisible by 400. Not every country [[agreed|baked|borrowed|prayed]] at once, because the change came from the Pope. Britain and its colonies waited until 1752, by which time eleven days had to be dropped, and crowds complained that the government had stolen them. Russia changed only in 1918, which is why its October Revolution actually happened in November. So the [[calendar|clock|century|tomorrow]] on your wall is a political document as much as an astronomical one. It will go wrong again, but the error is only about one day in three thousand years, so nobody in this [[century|calendar|clock|yesterday]] needs to worry.

## 二月為什麼有時候多一天
Why does February sometimes have twenty-nine days? Because a year is not a whole number of days. The Earth takes about 365.2422 days to travel once around the sun, so a [[calendar|clock|century|yesterday]] of 365 days falls behind by nearly six hours every year. Adding one day every four years almost fixes the problem, but it overshoots slightly, so the rule has an exception and the exception has an exception. The year 2000 was a leap year; 1900 was not, and 2100 will not be either. That is the rule about the end of a [[century|calendar|clock|tomorrow]]: skip the extra day unless the year divides by 400. People born on 29 February have a real birthday only once every four years, and most of them [[celebrate|bake|borrow|pray]] on the 28th or on 1 March instead. Computers get this wrong surprisingly often: in 2024 some shop tills and even a few car models refused to work on the extra day. If you want to test somebody's arithmetic, ask whether the year 2400 will be a leap year and watch them reach for a [[calendar|clock|century|yesterday]]. The answer is yes — which makes it a problem for the twenty-fifth [[century|calendar|clock|tomorrow]], not for us.

> 2 the rule about the end of a century「關於世紀末的規則」。calendar、clock、tomorrow 都不是一百年的單位。
> 3 most of them celebrate on the 28th「大多數人在二十八日慶生」。bake、borrow、pray 都接不上 on the 28th。
> 4 reach for a calendar「去拿日曆」。clock、century、yesterday 都不是用來查哪一年是閏年的工具。
> 5 a problem for the twenty-fifth century「二十五世紀的問題」。calendar、clock、tomorrow 都不能用序數加上年代這樣說。
+ whole number = 整數
+ overshoot = 超過

+ till = 收銀機

= 二月為什麼有時候有二十九天？因為一年不是整數天。地球繞太陽一圈大約要 365.2422 天，所以一部 365 天的曆法每年會落後將近六小時。每四年加一天幾乎解決了問題，但又稍微過頭，於是規則有例外，例外還有例外。2000 年是閏年；1900 年不是，2100 年也不會是。這就是關於世紀末的規則：除非年份能被 400 整除，否則不加那一天。二月二十九日出生的人每四年才有一次真正的生日，大多數人改在二十八日或三月一日慶生。電腦在這件事上出錯的頻率高得驚人：2024 年有些收銀機、甚至少數車款在多出來的那天拒絕運作。如果你想考考別人的算術，就問 2400 年是不是閏年，看他伸手去拿日曆。答案是「是」——所以那是二十五世紀的問題，不是我們的。

## 二十一世紀從哪一年開始
When did the twenty-first [[century|calendar|clock|tomorrow]] begin? Most of the world celebrated on the night of 31 December 1999, but the careful answer is 1 January 2001. The reason is that our counting of years has no year zero: the first century ran from the year 1 to the year 100, so the twentieth ran from 1901 to 2000, and the twenty-first began in 2001. Exactly the same argument broke out a [[century|calendar|clock|yesterday]] earlier, in 1899, and newspapers printed furious letters from both sides. In the end people celebrate the moment when the first three digits [[change|bake|borrow|pray]], not the date a mathematician would choose. Historians rarely care at all; they cut the past into useful pieces instead, so you will read about a "long nineteenth century" running from 1789 to 1914. A [[century|calendar|clock|tomorrow]] is only a word we agreed on, like a week. Still, if you ever want a safe argument with an adult, ask which [[calendar|century|clock|yesterday]] year the twenty-first century started in, and then say nothing.

## 每個細胞裡的鐘
Nearly every living thing has an inner [[clock|calendar|century|tomorrow]]. In the 1970s scientists noticed that fruit flies with one damaged gene lost their daily rhythm completely. In 1984 three American researchers managed to copy that gene, and over the following years they worked out what it does: a protein builds up inside the cell during the night and is broken down during the day, so its rising and falling acts like a small [[machine|calendar|century|yesterday]] inside every cell. In 2017 the three of them received the Nobel Prize for this work. Plants, flies, mice and people all run versions of the same system, which is why a long flight leaves you hungry at strange times, and why night-shift workers struggle: the body's [[clock|calendar|century|yesterday]] can be shifted, but only slowly, roughly an hour a day. Light is the main signal it listens to, which is why a bright screen at one in the morning can persuade your brain that [[tomorrow|yesterday|century|calendar]] has not arrived yet. Researchers now think some medicines work better at particular times of day, so one day a prescription may come with a [[clock|calendar|century|yesterday]] printed on it.
> 1 an inner clock「內在的時鐘」，也就是生理時鐘。calendar、century、tomorrow 都不是生物體內的構造。
> 2 acts like a small machine inside every cell「像每個細胞裡的小機器一樣運作」。calendar、century、yesterday 都不是細胞裡的東西。
> 3 the body's clock can be shifted「身體的時鐘可以被調整」。calendar、century、yesterday 都不能被「一天移動一小時」。
> 4 persuade your brain that tomorrow has not arrived yet「讓大腦以為明天還沒到」。yesterday 已經過去，century、calendar 都接不上 has not arrived。
> 5 a prescription may come with a clock printed on it「處方上可能印著一個時鐘」。calendar、century、yesterday 都不是標示服藥時間的圖示。
+ gene = 基因
+ rhythm = 節律
+ protein = 蛋白質
+ night shift = 夜班

= 幾乎每一種生物都有內在的時鐘。1970 年代，科學家注意到有一個基因受損的果蠅完全失去日節律。1984 年，三位美國研究者成功複製出那個基因，並在接下來幾年弄清楚它的作用：一種蛋白質在夜間於細胞內累積、白天被分解，它的升降就像每個細胞裡的一台小機器。2017 年，這三人因此獲得諾貝爾獎。植物、果蠅、老鼠和人類都運行著同一套系統的不同版本，這就是為什麼長途飛行會讓你在奇怪的時間覺得餓，也是為什麼夜班工作者很辛苦：身體的時鐘可以被調整，但很慢，大約一天一小時。它主要聽光的訊號，所以凌晨一點的明亮螢幕，能讓你的大腦以為明天還沒到。研究者現在認為，有些藥在一天中的特定時段效果更好，所以將來處方上也許會印著一個時鐘。

## 你真的記得昨天嗎
Try to recall exactly what you ate [[yesterday|tomorrow|century|calendar]] at lunch, and who said what while you ate it. Most people cannot, and that is perfectly normal. Memory is not a recording; it is rebuilt every time you use it, and the rebuilding can go wrong. In a famous experiment, people who were asked how fast two cars were going when they "smashed" into each other gave higher speeds than people asked when the cars "hit" each other — the same film, a different word. Researchers have even been able to make volunteers [[remember|bake|borrow|pray]] details that never happened at all, simply by asking the question in a particular way. That is why police are trained to ask open questions, and why two completely honest witnesses can describe [[yesterday|tomorrow|century|calendar]] differently without either of them lying. This is useful in ordinary life too. When you and a friend argue about who promised what, both of you may be [[wrong|salty|crowded|windy]]. Writing things down helps, which is one reason a diary or a [[calendar|clock|century|tomorrow]] is more trustworthy than your own confidence.
> 1 what you ate yesterday at lunch「你昨天午餐吃了什麼」。tomorrow 還沒發生，century、calendar 都不是時間點。
> 2 make volunteers remember details that never happened「讓受試者記得從未發生的細節」。bake、borrow、pray 都接不上 details。
> 3 can describe yesterday differently「對昨天的描述可能不同」。tomorrow 還沒發生，century、calendar 都不是目擊者描述的對象。
> 4 both of you may be wrong「你們兩個可能都記錯」。salty、crowded、windy 都不能形容記憶對錯。
> 5 a diary or a calendar is more trustworthy「日記或日曆比較可靠」。clock、century、tomorrow 都不能用來記下約定。
+ recall = 回想
+ rebuild = 重建
+ volunteer = 受試者、志願者
+ witness = 目擊者
+ trustworthy = 可信的
= 試著回想你昨天午餐吃了什麼，以及吃的時候誰說了什麼。大多數人想不起來，而這完全正常。記憶不是錄影；你每次使用它，它就被重建一次，而重建可能出錯。在一個著名的實驗裡，被問「兩輛車『撞爛』時速度多少」的人，給出的速度比被問「兩輛車『碰到』時速度多少」的人更高——同一段影片，不同的動詞。研究者甚至能靠特定的問法，讓受試者記得根本沒發生過的細節。這就是為什麼警察受訓要問開放式問題，也是為什麼兩個完全誠實的目擊者，可以對昨天有不同描述，而兩人都沒有說謊。這在日常生活也有用。當你和朋友為了「誰答應了什麼」爭執時，你們兩個可能都記錯。把事情寫下來有幫助，這也是日記或日曆比你自己的自信更可靠的原因之一。

## 為什麼年紀越大時間越快
Ask a child how long the summer holiday is, and then ask an adult. Children live as if there were an endless supply of [[tomorrow|yesterday|century|calendar]]; adults complain that the years have started to slide past. Part of the explanation is simple arithmetic. For a seven-year-old, one year is a seventh of everything they can remember; for a fifty-year-old it is a fiftieth, so it feels proportionally smaller. Psychologists add a second reason: novelty. Your brain stores new experiences in detail and old ones as summaries, so one week of travelling leaves more memories than a month of the same [[habit|garbage|traffic|weather]]. This leads to a practical trick. Put at least one genuinely new thing in your [[calendar|clock|century|yesterday]] every month, however small: a different route home, an unfamiliar food, a skill you are bad at. People who do this report that a year feels long again. It also suggests that a long life is not only about reaching a [[century|calendar|clock|tomorrow]]; it is about having something to remember from each part of it. Otherwise the weeks blur together, and you cannot even say what you did [[yesterday|tomorrow|century|calendar]], let alone last March.
> 1 an endless supply of tomorrow「無窮無盡的明天」。yesterday 是過去，century、calendar 都不能用 supply of 這樣搭配。
> 2 the same habit「同樣的習慣（一成不變的生活）」。garbage、traffic、weather 都不能和 the same 加上「一個月」這樣對比。
> 3 Put one new thing in your calendar every month「每個月在行事曆上放一件新的事」。clock、century、yesterday 都不是可以寫進去的東西。
> 4 reaching a century「活到一百歲」。calendar、clock、tomorrow 都不是人可以「到達」的長度。
> 5 what you did yesterday「你昨天做了什麼」。tomorrow 還沒發生，century、calendar 都不是時間點。
+ endless = 無盡的
+ proportionally = 按比例地
+ novelty = 新鮮感
+ unfamiliar = 不熟悉的
+ blur = 模糊成一片
= 問一個小孩暑假有多長，再問一個大人。小孩活得好像明天永遠用不完；大人則抱怨年歲開始飛逝。一部分解釋是簡單的算術。對七歲的孩子來說，一年是他記得的一切的七分之一；對五十歲的人來說只有五十分之一，比例上就顯得短。心理學家補上第二個原因：新鮮感。大腦把新經驗存成細節，把舊經驗存成摘要，所以一週的旅行留下的記憶，比一個月一成不變的生活還多。這帶出一個實用的方法：每個月在行事曆上放進至少一件真正新鮮的事，再小都好——換一條回家的路、一種沒吃過的食物、一項你很不擅長的技能。這樣做的人說，一年又變長了。它也暗示，長壽不只是活到一百歲，而是在生命的每個階段都有事情可以記得。否則週與週會模糊成一片，你連昨天做了什麼都說不出來，更別說去年三月。

## 二十四秒的誕生
In 1950 a professional basketball game in America finished 19–18. Teams with a lead simply kept the ball and waited, crowds stopped buying tickets, and the sport was in trouble. Then the owner of the team in Syracuse worked something out. He looked at the games he had actually enjoyed and noticed that the two sides together took about 120 shots. A game lasts 2,880 seconds, and 2,880 divided by 120 is 24. So he proposed a [[clock|calendar|century|tomorrow]] that gave a team 24 seconds to get a shot away, or lose the ball. The league tried it for the 1954–55 season and the effect was instant: average team scoring rose from 79.5 points a game to 93.1. It is a rare example of one small [[change|garbage|traffic|weather]] rescuing an entire sport, and nobody has seriously suggested removing it since. Today a 24-second [[clock|calendar|century|yesterday]] sits above every backboard, and good players read it without turning their heads. Basketball before that must have looked like a different game — closer to chess. Which is worth remembering when somebody says the rules of a sport are sacred: the thing that feels timeless was invented in the middle of the last [[century|calendar|clock|tomorrow]] by a man who had simply [[counted|baked|borrowed|prayed]] the shots.
> 1 he proposed a clock that gave a team 24 seconds「他提議一個給球隊 24 秒的計時器」。calendar、century、tomorrow 都不能用來限制進攻時間。
> 2 one small change rescuing an entire sport「一個小改變救了整項運動」。garbage、traffic、weather 都不能拯救一項運動。
> 3 a 24-second clock sits above every backboard「每個籃板上方都有一個 24 秒計時器」。calendar、century、yesterday 都不會裝在籃板上。
> 4 the middle of the last century「上個世紀中葉」。calendar、clock、tomorrow 都不是年代。
> 5 a man who had simply counted the shots「一個只是數了出手次數的人」。baked、borrowed、prayed 都接不上 the shots。
+ lead = 領先
+ league = 聯盟
+ instant = 立即的
+ backboard = 籃板
+ sacred = 神聖不可動的
= 1950 年，美國一場職業籃球比賽以 19 比 18 結束。領先的球隊就是抱著球等時間過去，觀眾不再買票，這項運動陷入危機。後來，雪城那支球隊的老闆算出了一件事。他看了自己真正看得開心的比賽，發現兩隊合計大約出手 120 次。一場比賽是 2,880 秒，2,880 除以 120 等於 24。於是他提議設一個計時器，給每隊 24 秒內出手，否則失去球權。聯盟在 1954–55 球季試行，效果立竿見影：平均每隊得分從 79.5 分升到 93.1 分。這是「一個小改變救了整項運動」的罕見例子，之後也沒有人認真提議取消它。今天每個籃板上方都有一個 24 秒計時器，好球員不用轉頭就知道剩幾秒。在那之前的籃球一定像另一種運動，比較接近西洋棋。所以當有人說某項運動的規則神聖不可動時，值得想起這件事：那個感覺「自古如此」的東西，是上個世紀中葉一個只是數了出手次數的人發明的。

## 棒球裝上了計時器
Baseball has no [[clock|calendar|century|tomorrow]], which is part of its charm and also its problem. Games crept longer and longer, reaching an average of three hours and ten minutes in 2021. So in 2023 the league put a timer on the pitcher: fifteen seconds to throw with the bases empty, twenty with runners on, and the batter must be ready with eight seconds left. The effect was the largest in living memory. The average nine-inning game fell by twenty-four minutes, to two hours and forty, the shortest since 1985, and the number of games lasting over three and a half hours dropped from 390 to nine. Players were divided: many said the game [[improved|baked|borrowed|prayed]] and that they got home earlier, while others missed the slow moments when a pitcher could step back and think. Older fans argue that the slowness was the whole point, that baseball was the one sport where you could hold a conversation. Younger fans reply that they will not watch anything lasting four hours. The league chose the [[future|calendar|clock|yesterday]] over the past. So the sport that proudly had no [[clock|calendar|century|tomorrow]] for more than a [[century|calendar|clock|yesterday]] now has one on the field and another in the dugout.

> 2 many said the game improved「許多人說比賽變好了」。baked、borrowed、prayed 都不是球員對規則的評價。
> 3 chose the future over the past「選擇了未來而非過去」。calendar、clock、yesterday 都不能和 over the past 這樣對比。
> 4 the sport that had no clock「沒有時鐘的運動」。calendar、century、tomorrow 都不合這個對照。

+ pitcher = 投手

## 相隔一百年的巴黎

## 兩小時的那道牆
For a long time nobody believed the [[clock|calendar|century|tomorrow]] could show less than two hours for a marathon. In October 2019, in a park in Vienna, a Kenyan runner crossed the line in 1 hour 59 minutes and 40 seconds. He said he only wanted to show that it was possible. His own official best, set in Berlin in 2022, was 2 hours 1 minute 9 seconds, and other runners have gone faster since. Our PE teacher says the useful part of the story is not the shoes but the [[plan|garbage|traffic|weather]]: he ran almost every kilometre within two seconds of the same pace.

> 2 the event was not a normal race「這場活動不是正式比賽」。garbage、traffic、weather 都不能用 normal 加上「不算紀錄」來說明。
> 3 a question for tomorrow rather than for never「是明天的問題，而不是永遠做不到」。yesterday、century、calendar 都接不上這個對比。
> 4 lost roughly half an hour in a century「一百年內快了大約半小時」。calendar、clock、yesterday 都不是一百年。

## 千分之一秒的勝負

## 練習生的時鐘

## 阿嬤廚房裡的那本曆
